West Sound Democratic Socialists of America (West Sound DSA) stands in solidarity with Cuba, condemns the Trump Administration's  actions regarding Cuba, and recognizes that the US should immediately lift all economic sanctions on the country, allowing aid, food, and fuel to enter the country. Despite recent developments within Cuba’s government and its approval of a series of free market reforms under intense US pressure, there is no indication by the Trump Administration for sanctions relief.

The Cuban people have had to deal with an ongoing extreme fuel and food shortages, and economic crisis due to the naval blockade of oil and other essential goods imposed by the US government earlier this year. Collective punishment of an entire people constitutes an illegal breach of international law under Common Article 33 of the Fourth Geneva Convention, and therefore also in violation of the US War Crimes Act of 1996. The federal government’s justification of the blockade and crippling sanctions on Cuba and its populace is due to its status as a socialist country, and its refusal over decades to allow its resources to be purchased by US based companies.

It is apparent that the Trump Administration wishes to do to Cuba what it has done to Venezuela, and destroy what notion of sovereignty they have based on perceived injustices to capitalist forces. The Trump Administration, and notably the US Secretary of State, Marco Rubio, wish to return US Latin American Policy to that of the Cold War.

Cuba has been subject to economic warfare and occupation since the 1960s. During an address to the UN General Assembly in 1964, Ernesto “Che” Guevara, then head of the Cuban delegation, criticized US imperialism, the failure of the UN to adequately address South African Apartheid at the time, and 5 points the Cuban government saw as the need for peace in the Caribbean:

1. A halt to the economic blockade and all economic and trade pressures by the United States, in all parts of the world, against our country.
2. A halt to all subversive activities, launching and landing of weap- ons and explosives by air and sea, organization of mercenary invasions, infiltration of spies and saboteurs, acts all carried out from the territory of the United States and some accomplice countries.
3. A halt to pirate attacks carried out from existing bases in the United States and Puerto Rico.
4. A halt to all the violations of our airspace and our territorial waters by U.S. aircraft and warships.
5. Withdrawal from the Guantánamo naval base and return of the Cuban territory occupied by the United States.”

He ended his hour-long speech with the words:

> ¡Patria o muerte!

Homeland or death. These are the words of the Cuban revolutionary spirit, and within them describe a sentiment held by much of the global proletariat in the struggle for sovereignty and freedom. Largely, the imperialism and colonialism of the last half millenia has been motivated by capitalism and resource extraction. The economic growth of capitalist empires, like the United States, has depended on the domination of industry, especially in the global south. It is in acknowledging this history that we can begin to consider how to decouple our society from this exploitation, and thus work to dismantle key mechanisms that serve to perpetuate capitalism.

As citizens of the “imperial core”, it is often too easy to look at the failings of socialism abroad as the failings of ideals. The failings of socialist countries, of which there certainly are, cannot solely be placed at the feet of their own citizens who had sought to have a country free of the unjust conditions that motivated their revolutions in the first place. Similar and oftentimes harsher failings can be seen within our own country. The US is an overtly patriarchal society, with systemic policy shaped by racism and bigotry towards queer and trans folks. The Republican Party is increasingly casting aside the lessons of science, while simultaneously rolling back the rights of US citizens, largely fought for by marginalized peoples.

Yet, we witness the presence of bigotries within socialist countries be used to discredit the revolutionary movements that birthed them. This is done without recognition of the inherently worse conditions present before their revolutions took place. It is often used by both liberals, neoliberals, and the right-wing of this country (and other liberal western democracies) to paint progressives and leftists as ideologically inconsistent. How can one believe in socialism and LGBTQ+ rights when a socialist government like Cuba had for a long time suppressed the rights of queer folk? Nevermind its recent legalization of same-sex marriage in 2022 and advocacy for LGBTQ+ communities through the CENESEX education campaign, led by Mariela Castro, daughter of Raúl Castro and niece of Fidel Castro. How can one desire economic uplift through socialism when one need only look at the condition of the economy of Cuba, and the comparative poverty of its citizenry? It surely could not be because of decades of sanctions and global trade restrictions by the United States on Cuba.

Even democracy is used as a cudgel against countries like China and Cuba, citing their one-party communist systems as evidence of being anti-democratic. This is despite the process by which these parties organize internal elections and candidates funneled through their labor movements through unions of laborers, farmers, students, and teachers. Compare this to the US where often candidates for office of a given party were not selected via merit, but instead promoted through corporate interests and hand picked by establishment party members.

It is important to recognize that societal and governmental failings come from structural inequalities, and that historic intervention by the US has never intended to better any people’s government, but instead served to extract their labor and resources for ourselves. The socialist project should focus on material gains for people broadly, taking wins where it can and minimizing compromise. In time, as a workers' movement grows, we will recognize that systemic inequalities must be dismantled if we are to maintain those very wins people fought so righteously for. One thing is all but certain, no matter a country’s  form of government, be it socialist, capitalist, feudal or theocratic, there is little hope for safety or prosperity when facing overwhelming oppressive and external forces. Cuba has faced economic oppression by the US for over half a century; Palestine has faced violent ethnic cleansing, apartheid and genocide for nearly a century. Countries across the Global South, North Africa and Western Asia face similar challenges in the midst of continued imperialism by the US and other major global powers.


## Historical Context

The story of Cuba is the story of Latin America. Of indigenous peoples ethnically cleansed and genocided by colonial European powers. Of imposed feudalism and enforced poverty. Of revolutionary spirit among the peasantry and bourgeoisie, much like that attributed to the United States 250 years ago, to throw off their colonial chains, only for that belligerent neighbor to the north to impose its own interests while waving the banner of capitalism. Once the US had mostly completed its own genocidal campaign of manifest destiny, its bourgeoisie transformed the isolationist settler-country into an empire that would levy its might to extract resources and labor from Latin America and abroad in order to sustain its brand of liberal capitalism.

The US would significantly step up its involvement in the Americas in the years following the defeated Reconstruction efforts post our Civil War. Foreign policy would lean on the Monroe Doctrine of the early 19th century, making use of the warfare advances in the wake of the Industrial Revolution. The US would selectively support revolutionary movements that would ensure its regional authority over that of Spain. Select bourgeoisie from these newly liberated peoples were chosen to manage puppet states and democratic republics that were only democratic in name. The defeat of Spain after the Spanish-American war firmly established a new vassal state dynamic of countries to the United States across the Pacific Ocean, in Central America, and in the Caribbean. Within a span of 3 years, the US would replace Spain as the direct colonial power over Cuba, the Philippines, Puerto Rico, and Guam, as well as complete its annexation over the Kingdom of Hawai’i and attain sovereignty over a number of islands in the Samoan Archipelago, now known as American Samoa.

Shortly after, President Roosevelt would adopt the Roosevelt Corollary to the Monroe Doctrine, in part as a response to European intervention in Venezuela. This addition asserts the United State’s desire to intervene in the governments of any Latin American country it felt was acting in opposition to US interests. Soon after Roosevelt would preside over the creation of an “independent” Cuban state, whose independence hinged on concessions including the right for the US to intervene in Cuban affairs, the inability for Cuba to sign treaties or compacts with any other foreign power, and the right for the US to purchase or lease land, such as Guantánamo Bay.

Elsewhere, the Roosevelt Corollary would be used in conjunction with Roosevelt’s “Big Stick” philosophy throughout the early 20th century in an attempt to squash local dissent, especially in Latin America. The US would back the United Fruit Company in its extortionate and corrupt endeavors in Central America. These banana republics were the result of a monopolized industry relegating most of a country’s peasantry to work on plantations not dissimilar to the plantations that were the economic engine of chattel slavery. Other Latin American countries not controlled by United Fruit still faced severe repression and growing class divides, fueled by US colonial investment. Many of these countries would be used as political pawns in conflicts between the US and other European powers, such as Woodrow Wilson’s military occupation of Haiti enacting martial law and forced labor in the leadup to World War I and lasting for decades afterwards. Caribbean countries at this time became more and more economically indebted to the US.

By the 1930s, Cuba’s economy would be dominated by sugar cane production until the price of sugar collapsed amidst the Great Depression. During this time, the US outwardly adopted a non-interventionist stance in Latin America under President Franklin Delano Roosevelt known as the “Good Neighbor Policy”, where he also ended the military occupation of Haiti. While the US was struggling to manage its own economic downturn,  worker and military unrest in Cuba gave way to regime change, a subsequent military coup and junta, and the eventual election of Fulgencio Batista on the backs of a workers movement and the Democratic Socialist Coalition, promoting popular reforms from free access to healthcare and freedom of political affiliation, and a new democratic Cuban constitution.

Inspired by Marxist-Leninist workers revolts in Europe and Russia, peasant uprisings occurred across Latin America.  Some had headwinds of democratic reform enacted because of the mass movements by the people, signaling a rejection of colonialism and extractive capitalism. Following the end of World War II, the US would return to interventionism in an attempt to combat what it saw as the growing threat of communism and influence in Latin America by the Soviet Union. Amid this new Cold War, in 1947 the Central Intelligence Agency was formed. The US and CIA would instigate and bankroll paramilitaries, insurgencies, and adventurists throughout the latter half of the 20th century. The aim was to topple any legitimate democratic governments that were perceived to be too socialist or radical and a threat to the United State’s capital interests.

Back in Cuba, Fulgencio Batista would attempt to remain in control of the government beyond the term limits set by the constitution via a puppet candidate. This candidate would lose the election and Batista would subsequently move to Florida while remaining the head of the Cuban Army and becoming a senator for Cuba. He planned to return and run again as the constitution allowed for non-consecutively held terms, but his unpopularity among the electorate would drive him to enact a coup d’état with help from the US and several senior Cuban army officials in what would be known as the Batistazo. This military junta would dramatically roll back the rights given by the constitution and align itself with the interests of wealthy land owners, especially those of sugar plantations. Corruption among the government grew to such an extent that the vast majority of farmland was owned by foreign actors and exploitative industry thrived in Havana.

It was shortly after this coup that Fidel Castro would challenge Batista’s regime in the court of law, and when this failed he and his brother Raúl would kickstart the Cuban Revolution in an attack on the Moncada Barracks on the 26th of July, 1953. Fidel Castro garnered so much fame from his statements in court following his arrest that Batista would grant amnesty to those arrested in the assault, including Castro, and exile them from the country and into Mexico. In the following years, the US would continue to back and financially support Batista as his regime ramped up public executions of dissenters and conducted a widespread campaign of terror. He established a secret police force known as the Bureau for the Repression of Communist Activities.

Fidel Castro & Raúl Castro, with the help of Che Guevara, would return to Cuba and build up a guerilla warfare campaign through the 26th of July Movement that would lead to the overthrow of Fulgencio Batista and his regime. After which they would enact massive economic and banking reform,  agrarian land reform, and a mass literacy campaign. 

> ¡Patria o muerte, venceremos!

“Homeland or death, we will prevail!” Again, these words are emblematic of the struggle for Cuban sovereignty as well as the last 66 years of Cuban struggle, ever since they were first spoken by Fidel Castro following the explosion of “La Coubre”, a French freighter carrying munitions to port. Castro placed blame for the explosion, and the nearly 100 killed and over 200 wounded, on the United States. The US and CIA would continue to fund and back military adventurism and political terrorism on the island of Cuba. This policy culminated in the failed Bay of Pigs invasion following Cuba’s nationalization of on-shore oil assets. The Bay of Pigs invasion was a US based paramilitary group composed of US nationals and anti-communist Cubans living in the US. Following this, the US would not only continue to fund and back right-wing insurgencies, but would impose a 6 decade long sanction policy that has since economically devastated Cuba and its citizens and forced them into excessive isolation.

Despite this, the Cuban people have persevered and remained resilient in the face of such cruelty. Cuba has retained one of the highest literacy rates of nations in the world, nearing 100% continuously since the early 60s. Doctors in Cuba have contributed significantly to healthcare access in rural communities of Latin America through their Latin American School of Medicine (ELAM). The Trump administration has recently targeted these medical programs by offering incentives to Latin American countries like Honduras and Guatemala to halt these programs and expel Cuban doctors from their countries. The State Department under Marco Rubio is dangling promises of medical infrastructure modernization as part of these incentives, with the ultimate goal being to eliminate the influence of a socialist Cuban state elsewhere in Latin America.

While it cannot be factually asserted to know where Cuba would be were it not for decades of US sanctions and military intervention, but its future should be the project of the Cuban people themselves, free of sanctions and collective punishment by the United States.
